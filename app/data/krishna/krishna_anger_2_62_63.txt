TEACHING SUMMARY:
In Bhagavad Gita 2.62-2.63, Krishna describes a progression in which repeatedly dwelling on sense objects can lead to attachment; attachment can give rise to desire; frustrated desire can lead to anger. Anger can cloud understanding and disturb memory and sound judgement, leading a person toward harmful decisions.

PRACTICAL INTERPRETATION:
When anger rises, pause before acting or speaking. Notice what expectation or attachment may have been frustrated. Give yourself time to regain clarity before making a consequential decision. This is a practical application of the sequence described in the verses, not a claim that one technique is prescribed word-for-word there.
